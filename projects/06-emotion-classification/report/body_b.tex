\section{method}

\subsection{models}

\textbf{tf-idf + logistic regression.} word 1--2 grams and character 2--5 grams (200k features each, sublinear
tf), one balanced logistic regression per label. $C$ was picked from $\{1, 4, 16\}$ on validation ($C=1$).

\textbf{bigru + glove.} a simple regex tokenizer, 300-d GloVe 6B vectors \cite{glove} for every word that has
one (random init for the rest that appear at least twice), a single bidirectional GRU with 256 units per
direction, then max and mean pooling over time and a linear layer. embeddings are fine-tuned with a 10x
smaller learning rate. this is the classic ``deep learning before transformers'' baseline.

\textbf{teacher: roberta-large} \cite{roberta} (355M parameters) with a linear head over the \texttt{<s>}
token. i trained two of them, one with plain BCE and one with ASL ($\gamma_-=4$, $\gamma_+=0$, clip 0.05).
both use AdamW, peak lr $2\cdot10^{-5}$ with 6\% warmup and linear decay, layer-wise lr decay of 0.95 per
layer going down (so the embeddings get about $0.28\times$ the head's lr), batch 32, 4 epochs, fp16 mixed
precision. the epoch with the best validation macro-f1 is kept. averaging the two teachers' logits gives a
small ensemble, which is what the student is distilled from.

\textbf{student: distilroberta-base} \cite{distilbert} (82M parameters, 6 layers). i picked it over MiniLM
because it uses the same byte-level BPE tokenizer as roberta, which keeps casing and emojis instead of
mapping them to \texttt{[UNK]}, and after int8 quantization it fits the ~90 mb budget for the browser. it is
trained for 6 epochs at lr $6\cdot10^{-5}$ with llrd 0.9 and the loss
\[
  \mathcal{L} = \alpha\,\mathrm{BCE}(z_s, y) + (1-\alpha)\,T^2\,\mathrm{BCE}\big(z_s/T,\ \sigma(\bar z_t/T)\big),
  \qquad \alpha = 0.5,\ T = 2,
\]
where $\bar z_t$ is the mean of the two teachers' logits on the training set. as an ablation the same student
is also trained on hard labels only.

\subsection{decision rule and calibration}

the same post-processing is applied to every model so the comparison is fair. on the validation set i fit
one temperature $T$ (grid search on the BCE), then pick a threshold per label from 0.02 to 0.95 that
maximises that label's f1. at test time a label is predicted if its calibrated probability passes its
threshold; if no label passes, the one closest to its threshold (highest $p/t$) is predicted, since every
GoEmotions comment has at least one label. Ekman scores are the max over each group's members, with their own
thresholds tuned the same way.

ECE is computed over every (comment, label) pair with 15 bins. because 96\% of those pairs are easy negatives
near zero, this number is tiny for every model, so i also report ECE restricted to pairs where the model
gives more than 5\%, which is where calibration actually matters for the demo.

\subsection{export}

the student is exported with optimum \cite{optimum} to ONNX (opset 17), then quantized with onnxruntime's
dynamic int8 quantization (MatMul and Gather, so the 50k$\times$768 embedding matrix is int8 too). an fp16
copy is made for webgpu. parity with pytorch is checked on a few sentences with \texttt{labcore.parity}, and
the int8 model is re-evaluated on the full test set. the browser uses the int8 model's own temperature and
thresholds.

\subsection{compute}

the transformers were trained on a free colab T4 (fp16 only, no bf16). one roberta-large epoch takes about 7
minutes at 3.35 it/s, the full queue (two teachers, two students, the bigru) about \todo{X} minutes.
everything after training (tf-idf, evaluation, export, benchmarks, figures) ran on my laptop's cpu.
